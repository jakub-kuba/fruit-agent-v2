# Fruit Knowledge Base
*A comprehensive encyclopedic reference for 22 fruits — covering seasonality, production, origin, and key facts.*

---

## 1. Apple

**Overview:**
Apple (*Malus domestica*) is one of the most widely cultivated fruits in the world. It belongs to the rose family and comes in thousands of varieties, ranging from sweet to tart. Apples are consumed fresh, juiced, and processed into products such as cider and vinegar.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
The main harvest season in Europe runs from August to November, with peak availability in September and October. Outside of this window, apples are available year-round thanks to controlled-atmosphere storage facilities.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. China (by far the largest, producing over 50% of global supply)
2. United States
3. Turkey

**Top EU Producers:**
Poland, Italy, France

**Import Origin to Europe:**
Outside of the European season, apples are imported primarily from Chile, South Africa, and New Zealand.

**Did you know?**
There are over 7,500 known cultivars of apples grown worldwide, each with distinct flavour, texture, and colour profiles.

## 2. Banana

**Overview:**
Banana (*Musa* spp.) is a tropical fruit and one of the most consumed fruits globally. It is rich in natural sugars and potassium, and is available in both sweet (dessert) and starchy (plantain) varieties.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Bananas are available year-round in Europe as they are entirely imported. There is no domestic European production season.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. India
2. China
3. Indonesia

**Top EU Producers:**
Bananas are not commercially produced in continental Europe. The Canary Islands (Spain) and Madeira (Portugal) are the only EU territories with notable banana production.

**Import Origin to Europe:**
The vast majority of bananas imported to Europe come from Ecuador, Colombia, Costa Rica, and Cameroon.

**Did you know?**
The bananas sold in supermarkets are almost exclusively of the Cavendish variety, chosen for its durability during long-distance transport.

## 3. Blackberry

**Overview:**
Blackberry (*Rubus fruticosus*) is a wild and cultivated berry known for its deep purple-black colour and sweet-tart flavour. It grows naturally across Europe and is also cultivated commercially.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Blackberries are in season from July to September in most of Europe, with wild varieties ripening slightly later than cultivated ones.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. Mexico
2. United States
3. Serbia

**Top EU Producers:**
Serbia (candidate country), Poland, Germany

**Import Origin to Europe:**
Fresh blackberries are imported from Mexico and Morocco during the off-season months.

**Did you know?**
Blackberries are technically not berries in the botanical sense — they are aggregate fruits made up of many small drupelets.

## 4. Blueberry

**Overview:**
Blueberry (*Vaccinium corymbosum*) is a small, round berry native to North America. It has gained significant popularity in Europe over the past two decades due to its antioxidant properties and mild, sweet flavour.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
The European blueberry season runs from June to September, with peak availability in July and August.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. United States
2. Canada
3. Peru

**Top EU Producers:**
Poland, Germany, the Netherlands (largely grown in greenhouses)

**Import Origin to Europe:**
During winter months, blueberries are imported from Peru, Chile, and South Africa.

**Did you know?**
Poland has become one of the largest blueberry producers in Europe, with production increasing dramatically since the 2000s.

## 5. Cherry

**Overview:**
Cherry (*Prunus avium* for sweet, *Prunus cerasus* for sour) is a stone fruit enjoyed fresh or processed into jams, juices, and liqueurs. Sweet cherries are eaten raw while sour varieties are predominantly used in cooking and baking.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Cherries have a short and distinct season in Europe, typically from late May to August, depending on the region and variety.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. Turkey
2. United States
3. Iran

**Top EU Producers:**
Spain, Italy, Greece, Hungary, Poland

**Import Origin to Europe:**
During the off-season, cherries are imported from Chile and South Africa.

**Did you know?**
Turkey is the world's largest producer of cherries, accounting for approximately 20–25% of global production.

## 6. Chokeberry

**Overview:**
Chokeberry (*Aronia melanocarpa*) is a shrub berry native to North America, now widely cultivated in Eastern Europe. It is known for its intensely tart flavour and very high antioxidant content, making it popular in health food products, juices, and supplements.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Chokeberries are harvested in late summer and early autumn, typically from August to October.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. Poland
2. Russia
3. Denmark

**Top EU Producers:**
Poland is by far the dominant EU producer, responsible for the majority of global commercial production.

**Import Origin to Europe:**
Chokeberry is predominantly a European product and is rarely imported. Poland exports chokeberry products to other EU countries and beyond.

**Did you know?**
Poland produces an estimated 90% of the world's commercial chokeberry supply, making it a uniquely European-dominated fruit market.

## 7. Dark Grape

**Overview:**
Dark (red/black) grape (*Vitis vinifera*) refers to grape varieties with deep-coloured skin, used both for fresh consumption (table grapes) and wine production. They are cultivated extensively across the Mediterranean region.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
The European grape harvest, known as the vendange, takes place between August and October, varying by region and variety.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. China
2. Italy
3. Spain

**Top EU Producers:**
Italy, Spain, France, Greece, Portugal

**Import Origin to Europe:**
During the off-season, dark grapes are imported from South Africa, Chile, Peru, and India.

**Did you know?**
The colour of dark grapes comes from pigments called anthocyanins, which are concentrated in the skin of the fruit.

## 8. Ginger

**Overview:**
Ginger (*Zingiber officinale*) is a flowering plant whose rhizome (root) is widely used as a spice and flavouring agent. Though technically a root rather than a fruit, it is often categorised alongside fresh produce in markets and food retail.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Ginger is available year-round in Europe as it is entirely imported. There is no European production season.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. India
2. China
3. Nigeria

**Top EU Producers:**
Ginger is not commercially produced in Europe due to climate requirements.

**Import Origin to Europe:**
Ginger is imported to Europe primarily from India, China, Peru, and Brazil.

**Did you know?**
India alone accounts for approximately 40% of the world's total ginger production and is also its largest consumer.

## 9. Grapefruit

**Overview:**
Grapefruit (*Citrus paradisi*) is a subtropical citrus fruit known for its bitter-sweet flavour. It is a hybrid, believed to have originated in Barbados as a cross between pomelo and sweet orange. It is consumed fresh, juiced, and used in culinary applications.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Grapefruit is available year-round in Europe, with peak season from November to April when Northern Hemisphere production is at its highest.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. China
2. United States
3. Mexico

**Top EU Producers:**
Spain and Cyprus produce limited quantities, but the EU is largely dependent on imports.

**Import Origin to Europe:**
Grapefruits are imported primarily from Turkey, South Africa, Israel, and the United States.

**Did you know?**
Grapefruit can interact with over 85 medications, including statins and blood pressure drugs, by inhibiting a key enzyme involved in drug metabolism.

## 10. Hazelnut

**Overview:**
Hazelnut (*Corylus avellana*) is a tree nut widely used in confectionery, spreads (such as Nutella), and cooking. It has a rich, sweet flavour and is one of the most commercially significant nuts in the world.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Hazelnuts are harvested in Europe between August and October. They are available year-round due to long shelf life and storage capabilities.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. Turkey (dominates global supply)
2. Italy
3. Azerbaijan

**Top EU Producers:**
Italy (particularly Sicily and Campania), Spain, Georgia (candidate country)

**Import Origin to Europe:**
Turkey supplies the vast majority of hazelnuts consumed in Europe, accounting for roughly 70% of global production.

**Did you know?**
Approximately 70% of the world's hazelnuts come from Turkey's Black Sea coast region, making it one of the most geographically concentrated agricultural commodities in the world.

## 11. Lemon

**Overview:**
Lemon (*Citrus limon*) is a yellow citrus fruit prized for its sour juice and aromatic zest. It is one of the most versatile fruits in culinary use, appearing in beverages, desserts, sauces, and preserves worldwide.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Lemons are produced in Southern Europe year-round, with peak harvests typically occurring between November and May.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. India
2. Mexico
3. China

**Top EU Producers:**
Spain, Italy (Sicily is particularly renowned for its lemons)

**Import Origin to Europe:**
When domestic supply is insufficient, lemons are imported from Turkey, Argentina, and South Africa.

**Did you know?**
Sicily's Femminello variety of lemon is one of the few fruits that can flower and fruit simultaneously, allowing for multiple harvests per year.

## 12. Lime

**Overview:**
Lime (*Citrus aurantiifolia* or *Citrus latifolia*) is a small, green citrus fruit with an intensely sour flavour. It is widely used in beverages, cooking, and as a garnish, particularly in Latin American, Southeast Asian, and Middle Eastern cuisines.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Limes are not produced commercially in Europe and are available year-round as an imported product.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. India
2. Mexico
3. China

**Top EU Producers:**
Limes are not commercially grown in the EU.

**Import Origin to Europe:**
Limes imported to Europe come primarily from Mexico, Brazil, Peru, and the Dominican Republic.

**Did you know?**
British sailors were once given lime juice to prevent scurvy during long sea voyages, which is the origin of the slang term "limey" for British people.

## 13. Orange

**Overview:**
Orange (*Citrus sinensis*) is the world's most widely consumed citrus fruit. It is valued for its sweet juice, edible flesh, and aromatic peel. Oranges are consumed fresh and processed into juice, essential oils, and marmalades.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Southern European oranges are in season from November to April. Spain and Italy are key producers with overlapping but distinct seasonal windows.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. Brazil
2. China
3. India

**Top EU Producers:**
Spain, Italy, Greece

**Import Origin to Europe:**
During summer months, oranges are imported from South Africa, Egypt, and Morocco.

**Did you know?**
Brazil is the world's largest producer of oranges and accounts for approximately 50% of global orange juice production.

## 14. Peach

**Overview:**
Peach (*Prunus persica*) is a stone fruit native to Northwest China. It is characterised by its soft, juicy flesh and distinctive fuzzy skin. Peaches are consumed fresh and used in jams, canned products, and desserts.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Peaches are in season in Europe from June to September, with July and August representing the peak months.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. China
2. Spain
3. Italy

**Top EU Producers:**
Spain, Italy, Greece

**Import Origin to Europe:**
Peaches are rarely imported from outside the EU due to their short shelf life. South African peaches occasionally appear during the European winter.

**Did you know?**
China produces approximately 60% of the world's peaches, yet exports very little, as the vast majority is consumed domestically.

## 15. Pear

**Overview:**
Pear (*Pyrus communis*) is a sweet, mild fruit related to the apple. It comes in many varieties — from the soft and juicy Conference pear to the firm Bosc — and is consumed fresh, canned, or in juices and spirits such as perry.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
The European pear season runs from August to November, with some varieties available through controlled storage until early spring.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. China
2. Argentina
3. Italy

**Top EU Producers:**
Italy, Netherlands, Belgium, Spain

**Import Origin to Europe:**
During the off-season, pears are imported from Argentina, South Africa, and Chile.

**Did you know?**
The Netherlands is one of Europe's largest pear exporters despite its relatively small land area, due to highly optimised horticultural practices.

---

## 16. Plum

**Overview:**
Plum (*Prunus domestica*) is a stone fruit that comes in a wide variety of colours, from yellow and red to deep purple. It is consumed fresh and widely used in jams, dried form (prunes), juices, and spirits such as Slivovitz.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Plums are in season in Europe from July to October, with peak availability in August and September.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. China
2. Romania
3. Serbia

**Top EU Producers:**
Romania, Poland, Spain, Hungary

**Import Origin to Europe:**
South African and Chilean plums are imported during the European off-season (winter months).

**Did you know?**
Romania and Serbia together account for a significant share of European plum production, much of which is used to produce traditional fruit brandies.

## 17. Raspberry

**Overview:**
Raspberry (*Rubus idaeus*) is a delicate aggregate berry with a sweet-tart flavour. It is highly perishable and widely used fresh, frozen, and in jams, desserts, and beverages. Raspberries are one of the most commercially valuable soft fruits in Europe.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Raspberries are in season from June to October in Europe, with a main summer crop and sometimes an autumn crop from ever-bearing varieties.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. Russia
2. Mexico
3. Serbia

**Top EU Producers:**
Poland, Serbia (candidate), Germany, Spain

**Import Origin to Europe:**
Morocco and Chile supply raspberries to the European market during the off-season.

**Did you know?**
Poland is one of the largest raspberry producers in the EU and a major exporter of frozen raspberries, supplying food manufacturers across Europe.

## 18. Strawberry

**Overview:**
Strawberry (*Fragaria × ananassa*) is one of the most popular and widely consumed fruits in Europe. Known for its bright red colour, sweet aroma, and juicy flesh, it is eaten fresh and used extensively in confectionery, jams, and dairy products.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
The main strawberry season in Europe runs from April to July, starting in southern countries (Spain, Italy) and moving northward. Greenhouse production extends availability beyond the natural season.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. China
2. United States
3. Mexico

**Top EU Producers:**
Spain, Poland, Germany, Italy

**Import Origin to Europe:**
Morocco is the primary source of imported strawberries during early spring, before the European season begins.

**Did you know?**
Spain's Huelva region produces around 90% of Spain's strawberries and is one of the most intensive strawberry-growing areas in the world.

## 19. Tangerine

**Overview:**
Tangerine (*Citrus reticulata*) is a small, easy-to-peel citrus fruit with a sweet, mild flavour. It is closely related to the mandarin and is a popular snack fruit, especially during the winter months in Europe.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Tangerines are in season in Europe from October to March, making them a quintessential winter fruit on European markets.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. China
2. Spain
3. Turkey

**Top EU Producers:**
Spain, Italy, Greece

**Import Origin to Europe:**
Morocco, Turkey, and Egypt are key suppliers of tangerines to European markets, particularly early and late in the season.

**Did you know?**
The name "tangerine" comes from Tangier, the Moroccan port city from which these fruits were historically shipped to Europe.

## 20. Walnut

**Overview:**
Walnut (*Juglans regia*) is a tree nut with a rich, slightly bitter flavour and a hard shell. It is widely used in baking, confectionery, and cooking, and is valued for its high content of omega-3 fatty acids.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Walnuts are harvested in Europe between September and November. Due to their long shelf life, they are available year-round.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. China
2. United States
3. Iran

**Top EU Producers:**
France, Romania, Hungary, Greece

**Import Origin to Europe:**
The United States (particularly California) and Ukraine are significant suppliers of walnuts to European markets.

**Did you know?**
California produces approximately 99% of the commercially grown walnuts in the United States and is a major global exporter.

## 21. Watermelon

**Overview:**
Watermelon (*Citrullus lanatus*) is a large fruit with a thick green rind and sweet, watery red (or occasionally yellow) flesh. It is one of the most consumed fruits during summer in Europe and is almost entirely made up of water, making it a popular refreshment.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
Watermelons are in season in Europe from June to September, with peak availability in July and August.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. China
2. Turkey
3. Iran

**Top EU Producers:**
Spain, Greece, Italy, Romania

**Import Origin to Europe:**
Early in the season, watermelons are imported from Morocco and Senegal. Outside of the season, Brazil and Senegal supply the European market.

**Did you know?**
China produces approximately 70% of the world's watermelons, more than all other countries combined.

---

## 22. White Grape

**Overview:**
White (green) grape (*Vitis vinifera*) refers to grape varieties with pale green to golden skin. Like dark grapes, they are used both as table grapes for fresh consumption and as the basis for white and sparkling wines. They tend to have a lighter, crisper flavour than dark varieties.

**Seasonality in Europe:**
White grapes are harvested in Europe between August and October, with timing varying by variety and region.

**Top 3 World Producers:**
1. China
2. Italy
3. Spain

**Top EU Producers:**
Italy, Spain, France, Portugal, Greece

**Import Origin to Europe:**
During the off-season, white grapes are sourced from South Africa, Chile, Peru, and India.

**Did you know?**
White grapes are used to produce some of the world's most prestigious wines, including Champagne, Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, and Riesling.
